How equity gains are taxed
  • Held 12 months or less: short-term, taxed at 20% of the gain.
  • Held longer: long-term, taxed at 12.5% of the gains above ₹1,25,000 in a year.
  • Plus 4% cess on the tax. PPF interest is tax-free; FD and RD interest is taxed every year at your slab rate.
  • The amounts above already have this tax taken off.

The SIP's value moves with the market; PPF and the RD do not.

If the fund does better or worse

    How this works

    The tool puts the same amount in each, at the start of every month, for the same years. The SIP grows at the fund return you expect and is sold at the end, with capital-gains tax: instalments from the last year are short-term, the rest long-term above the yearly exemption. PPF earns its rate on the balance each month, credited once a year, and is tax-free. The recurring deposit grows at its rate after your tax on the interest each year.

    Questions

    Why does PPF often beat the recurring deposit?

    PPF interest is tax-free, while RD interest is taxed every year at your rate. At similar rates, the tax makes the RD grow more slowly.

    Why is part of the SIP taxed as short-term?

    Each instalment is its own purchase. When you sell everything at the end, instalments held a year or less count as short-term and are taxed at a higher rate; the rest are long-term, taxed above the yearly exemption.

    What does the tool leave out?

    The PPF deduction under the old tax regime, changes to the PPF rate, fund expense ratios beyond what the return you enter already reflects, and surcharge on high incomes. If any of these matter for you, adjust the inputs.
