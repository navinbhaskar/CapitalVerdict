How equity gains are taxed
  • Held 12 months or less: short-term, taxed at 20% of the gain.
  • Held longer: long-term, taxed at 12.5% of the gains above ₹1,25,000 in a year.
  • Plus 4% cess on the tax. PPF interest is tax-free; FD and RD interest is taxed every year at your slab rate.
  • This tool asks for your return after tax. For an equity fund held for years, that is lower than its return before tax: in the plot vs FD vs fund example, 10% a year before tax keeps about 9.1% a year after tax over 10 years. For a fixed deposit, it is the rate × (1 − your tax rate).

    How this works

    The tool follows two households with the same money. One puts the amount into the loan and invests whatever that frees up each month: the whole EMI once the loan ends early, or the drop in EMI. The other invests the amount and keeps paying the loan as it is. Both spend the same each month. When the loan's original end date comes, neither owes anything, so the one with more invested is ahead.

    Questions

    Why compare at the loan's original end date?

    By then both households owe nothing, so what each has invested is its whole position. Comparing earlier would mean weighing a smaller loan against a bigger investment pot, which hides the answer.

    Why does the return after tax matter so much?

    Prepaying earns exactly the loan rate, with no risk and no tax. Investing has to beat that after tax to come out ahead. The tool shows the return at which the two are level.

    Is the tax benefit on home-loan interest included?

    Only if you enter it. Whether you can deduct interest, and up to what limit, depends on your tax regime and the rules for the year, so the tool asks for your tax rate and the limit instead of assuming them. Prepaying lowers the interest, so it can lower this benefit too.
