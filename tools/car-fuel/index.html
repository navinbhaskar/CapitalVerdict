How this works

Each car’s monthly fuel cost is distance ÷ mileage × fuel price, plus any extra upkeep. The CNG car’s saving adds up month by month, and it breaks even when that running total covers the extra price of the CNG variant. The stricter test also charges interest on the extra price, at your loan rate or what the money could have earned, compounded monthly. Fuel prices rise each year by the rates you set; by default they stay flat.

Questions

Why are there two break-even figures?

Without interest, break-even is when the fuel saved equals the extra price. With interest, the extra price also grows at your loan rate, because that money was borrowed or could have earned elsewhere. The second figure comes later, and the verdict uses it.

Where do the fuel prices come from?

Choose your city under Assumptions. Verified city prices fill in automatically, with their source and date in the table above. Prices change daily, so check your local pump.

Does it include resale value, insurance or kit servicing?

Resale value and insurance are not included. Add any extra yearly running cost, such as cylinder testing or kit servicing, under Extra upkeep.

What if I drive less than the example?

The saving shrinks with distance. “What would flip the answer” shows the monthly distance below which petrol wins with your numbers.
