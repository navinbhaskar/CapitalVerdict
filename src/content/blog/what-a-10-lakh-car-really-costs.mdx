A car's sticker price is the start of what it costs, not the end. The biggest costs never arrive as a bill: the car loses value, and the money in it stops earning. Here is a ₹10 lakh car, ex-showroom, registered in Delhi, paid for in cash and kept 5 years.

## The money that goes out on day one

- **₹10 lakh** for the car.
- **₹70,000** road tax. Delhi charges 7% of the price for cars from ₹6 lakh to ₹10 lakh, once, at registration.
- Registration fees, insurance and accessories on top, which vary by dealer and insurer.

Watch the ₹10 lakh line: above it, Delhi's rate jumps to 10% of the whole price. A car priced at ₹10,00,001 pays **₹1,00,000** of road tax, ₹30,000 more for one rupee.

## What it is worth as it ages

When insurers set a car's insured value, they take a fixed share off the list price by its age. It is not a market resale price, and used cars often sell for more or less, but it is the one official yardstick.

## What the cash could have earned

The ₹10.70 lakh paid out on day one could have stayed invested. At 7% a year after tax, it would have grown by **₹4.31 lakh** in 5 years. That is a real cost of owning the car, even though no one sends you a bill for it.

That comes to about **₹10.18 lakh** over 5 years, or **₹16,964** a month. Fuel, servicing, parking and comprehensive insurance come on top. Third-party cover alone is ₹3,416 a year before GST for a 1,000 to 1,500 cc car; comprehensive cover costs more and depends on the insurer.

## On a loan instead

Borrow 80% at 9% over 5 years with a ₹5,000 fee, and the EMI is **₹16,607**. You pay **₹1.96 lakh** in interest. If your savings earn 7% after tax, you end up **₹67,298** behind someone who paid cash and invested the EMI instead. Our guide [Car loan or cash](/guides/car-loan-or-cash/) explains why: with the fee, the loan really costs 9.67% a year, more than the savings earn.

## Ways to bring the cost down

- **Keep it longer.** The first year takes the biggest bite, 15% of the price; after that the schedule takes 5% to 10% a year.
- **Mind the ₹10 lakh line** in Delhi, where the road tax rate steps up from 7% to 10%.
- **Pay cash if your savings earn less than the loan costs.** Our [cash or car loan calculator](/tools/cash-or-car-loan/) shows it for your own loan and returns.
- **Consider a used car**: the first owner has already paid the steepest part of the fall in value, and the road tax. Our [new or used car calculator](/tools/new-or-used-car/) compares the two for your prices.
