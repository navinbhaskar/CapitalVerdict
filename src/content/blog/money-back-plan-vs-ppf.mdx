A money-back plan is a life insurance policy that returns part of what you paid at set points along the way, then a larger sum at the end. Getting money back every five years is reassuring. But the payments are your own premiums coming back, and what matters is how much they grew on the way.

## The example plan

This is the money-back example in our [plan return calculator](/tools/plan-return/). It is an illustration, not any real plan's figures.

- You pay **₹40,000** a year for 15 years: **₹6 lakh** in all.
- It pays **₹1 lakh** at the end of years 5, 10 and 15, and **₹6 lakh** at the end of year 20: **₹9 lakh** in all.

₹9 lakh back for ₹6 lakh paid sounds like a 50% gain. But it is spread over 20 years. The one yearly rate at which your premiums grow into exactly those payouts, the plan's return, is about **4.4%** a year. After 6% inflation that is about **−1.5%** a year: the payouts buy less than the premiums did.

This compares rates, not products. PPF has its own rules on when money can come out before it matures, so it won't hand back ₹1.25 lakh in year 5 the way this plan pays ₹1 lakh. What the comparison shows is how much growth the plan gives up to make those payments on schedule.

## You get your money back last

By the end of year 15 you have paid ₹6 lakh and had ₹3 lakh back. You only get all your premiums back with the final ₹6 lakh in year 20. Until then, stopping the plan means taking its surrender value, which can be far below what you paid.

## What a money-back plan does give you

- **Life cover** for the policy term. If you need cover, compare the plan with a term plan for the same cover plus investing the rest: our [term plus investing calculator](/tools/term-plus-investing/) does exactly that.
- **Fixed dates** for the payouts, if you have known expenses at those points.
- **No market risk** on the guaranteed amounts.

## Before you buy one

- Enter only the amounts the plan **guarantees** in the plan return calculator. Bonuses in a benefit illustration are not promised.
- Look at the return a year, not the total it pays back.
- Ask what you get if you stop paying after a few years.
