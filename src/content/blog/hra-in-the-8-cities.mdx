- From tax year 2026-27, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad join Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai at the 50% HRA limit, up from 40%.
- It helps only renters in those four cities, under the old tax regime, whose rent is high for their salary.
- On ₹8 lakh a year of basic pay with ₹4 lakh of HRA, at ₹40,000 rent, ₹80,000 more is exempt: **₹24,960** less tax a year at the 30% rate. Below ₹33,333 rent it changes nothing.

House rent allowance is part of many salaries. Under the old regime, some of it is free of tax if you pay rent. The Income-tax Rules, 2026 (Rule 279) changed one of the limits from tax year 2026-27, which began on 1 April 2026.

## How the exemption works

The tax-free part of your HRA is the **least** of three amounts:

1. The HRA you receive.
2. The rent you pay, minus 10% of your salary.
3. 50% of your salary in the eight cities, or 40% anywhere else.

"Salary" here means basic pay plus dearness allowance where your terms of employment provide it, without your other allowances.

## What changed

Until tax year 2025-26, only Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai had the 50% limit. From 2026-27 it also covers **Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad**. Everywhere else stays at 40%.

So the change raises only the third limit, and only in four cities. It helps you only when that third limit was the smallest of the three: when both your HRA and your rent were high enough that 40% of salary capped the exemption.

## One salary, five rents

Take ₹8 lakh a year of basic pay and DA, and ₹4 lakh of HRA, in one of the four cities.

- At **₹30,000** rent or less, nothing changes: rent minus 10% of salary was already below 40% of salary, so it set the limit.
- From about **₹33,333** a month, rent minus 10% of salary passes 40% of salary, and the higher limit starts to count.
- At **₹40,000**, the exempt amount rises from ₹3.20 lakh to ₹4 lakh: **₹80,000** more, or **₹24,960** less tax a year.
- Above that, the HRA you receive caps it. On this salary the change can never add more than ₹80,000, a tenth of salary.

## Who gains, in short

You gain only if all of these are true:

- You live in Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune or Ahmedabad, and pay rent.
- You file under the old tax regime.
- Your HRA is more than 40% of your basic pay and DA.
- Your rent is more than half of your basic pay and DA, over the year.

## Before you count on it

- The gain is a reason to look at the old regime again, not a reason to switch. Our [old or new tax regime calculator](/tools/old-or-new-tax-regime/) takes your HRA and rent and shows which regime leaves less tax.
- Keep rent receipts and the rental agreement; your employer may ask for them.
- Tell your employer which city you live in, so they apply the right limit through the year.
