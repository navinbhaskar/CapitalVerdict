<InShort>

- A systematic withdrawal plan (SWP) sells a little of your fund every month to pay you an income, while the rest stays invested.
- If the income rises 6% a year with prices, ₹50 lakh at an illustrative 10% a year can pay about **₹26,409** a month for 25 years.
- ₹30,000 a month rising the same way runs out after about 20 years. The tax on the withdrawals is small.

</InShort>

An SWP is the reverse of a SIP. Instead of putting a fixed amount in every month, you take a fixed amount out. The rest of the money stays invested and keeps growing, so the withdrawals can last a long time. The question is how much you can take without running out.

## Flat withdrawals look generous

Put ₹50 lakh in an equity fund at an illustrative 10% a year and take out ₹30,000 a month. Kept at ₹30,000, the withdrawals last all 25 years, with ₹1.72 crore still left at the end.

But a flat ₹30,000 buys less every year. At 6% inflation, ₹30,000 at the end of year 25 buys about what ₹6,990 buys today. If the withdrawals are your living costs, they need to rise with prices.

## Withdrawals that keep up with prices

Raise the ₹30,000 by 6% every year, and the money runs out after **20 years 5 months**. By then you have taken out ₹1.38 crore.

The most that ₹50 lakh can pay for the full 25 years, rising 6% a year, is **₹26,409** a month to start. That rises to ₹1,06,928 a month by year 25.

At 7% a year, the starting income falls to ₹19,302 a month. Nobody knows the return you will get, so plan with a cautious one.

## The tax is low

Each withdrawal sells units, and only the gain in those units is taxed. Early on, most of each withdrawal is your own money coming back, so the tax is small. Over the 20 years that ₹30,000 rising 6% lasts, the tax on the gains comes to about ₹8.33 lakh, on ₹1.38 crore withdrawn. Long-term gains up to ₹1.25 lakh a year are tax-free.

## The risk an average hides

These figures assume the same return every year. Real markets move up and down, and **the order matters**. A fall in the first few years, while you are also withdrawing, does far more damage than the same fall later. That is why many retirees keep a year or two of withdrawals somewhere safer, and take less than the maximum.
