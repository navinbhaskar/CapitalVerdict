- ELSS, PPF and a life insurance plan all count for 80C, so each saves the same tax: **₹31,200** a year on ₹1 lakh at the 30% rate.
- 80C helps only under the old tax regime. Under the new regime, the default, none of the three saves tax.
- What differs is what the money grows to. ₹1 lakh a year for 12 years: an endowment plan pays **₹20 lakh** by year 20, the same premiums at PPF's 7.1% give **₹33.36 lakh**, and an ELSS at an illustrative 10% gives about **₹45.59 lakh** after tax.

Section 80C lets you deduct up to ₹1.5 lakh a year from your taxable income for certain savings. Three of the most common are an ELSS (a tax-saving equity mutual fund), the Public Provident Fund, and a life insurance plan. Because each rupee counts the same for the deduction, the tax saving can't separate them. What separates them is what each rupee becomes.

## First: does 80C help you at all?

80C is an old-regime deduction. Under the new regime, which applies unless you choose the old one, it saves nothing. If the new regime leaves you with less tax, you can still save in any of these three, but the tax break is not a reason to. Our [old or new tax regime calculator](/tools/old-or-new-tax-regime/) shows which regime suits you.

## The same tax saving

On the old regime at the 30% rate, ₹1 lakh in 80C cuts your tax by ₹30,000, plus the 4% cess: **₹31,200** a year. Over 12 years that is **₹3.74 lakh**, whichever of the three you choose.

## What ₹1 lakh a year becomes

Take the endowment example from our [plan return calculator](/tools/plan-return/): ₹1 lakh a year for 12 years, ₹12 lakh in all, and ₹20 lakh paid back at the end of year 20. Its return works out to **3.5%** a year. It is an example, not any real plan's figures.

Now put the same ₹1 lakh, on the same dates, into the other two:

- **PPF at 7.1%**, tax-free: **₹33.36 lakh** at year 20. PPF matures after 15 years and can be extended in 5-year blocks, so the money can stay in.
- **ELSS at an illustrative 10%** a year before tax: ₹50.42 lakh at year 20. Selling it all that year, the gain above ₹1.25 lakh is taxed at 12.5% plus cess, ₹4.83 lakh, leaving about **₹45.59 lakh**. The 10% is not a forecast; equity funds can fall, and stay down for years.

## The differences beyond the return

<Table title="ELSS, PPF and an endowment plan side by side" table={eightyC.table} />

- **Lock-in.** ELSS units can't be sold for 3 years, and with a monthly SIP each instalment has its own 3 years. PPF runs 15 years. An insurance plan runs its full term; stopping early can return far less than you paid.
- **Certainty.** PPF's rate is set by the government each quarter. A plan pays only what it guarantees. An ELSS can end well above or well below the 10% used here.
- **Life cover.** Only the insurance plan has it. If you need cover, our [term plus investing calculator](/tools/term-plus-investing/) shows what a plan with cover costs compared with a term plan and investing the rest.

## Before you choose

- Check which tax regime suits you first. Under the new regime the choice is only about returns and lock-in.
- Count what already uses up your ₹1.5 lakh: your provident fund contributions and home-loan principal count too.
- For an insurance plan, enter its guaranteed amounts in the plan return calculator to see its own return, rather than the total it promises.
