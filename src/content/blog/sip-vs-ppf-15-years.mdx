PPF and an equity SIP are two of the most common ways to save every month. PPF pays a rate set by the government, is tax-free and cannot fall. A SIP in an equity fund can grow faster but is taxed when you sell, and its value goes up and down. To compare them fairly, compare what each **leaves you after tax**.

## The setup

₹10,000 at the start of every month for 15 years, PPF's full term. That is ₹18 lakh saved.

- **PPF** at **7.1%**, the rate for October–December 2026, held for the whole term. Interest is worked out monthly, credited once a year, and tax-free.
- **Equity SIP** at an illustrative **10% a year** before tax. Taxed when sold: short-term gains on the last year's instalments, long-term gains above ₹1.25 lakh.
- **Recurring deposit** at an illustrative **7%**, with the interest taxed every year at 31.2% (the 30% slab plus cess).

## What each leaves you

- **Equity SIP: ₹37.44 lakh**, after ₹2.72 lakh of tax.
- **PPF: ₹31.56 lakh**, tax-free.
- **Recurring deposit: ₹26.20 lakh**, after ₹5.09 lakh of tax on the interest.

At 10% a year, the SIP comes out about ₹5.88 lakh ahead. The recurring deposit trails because its interest is taxed every year, while PPF's is not taxed at all.

## The number that decides it

The SIP only wins if the fund does well enough. With these figures, it needs a return above **7.79%** a year before tax to beat PPF. At 7% a year, it would keep about ₹29.72 lakh, less than PPF.

So the real question is how confident you are of beating that rate, after costs, for 15 years. Nobody knows future returns; the calculator shows the answer at returns 2 points either side of yours.

## What the numbers leave out

- **Risk.** A SIP can be worth less than you put in for years at a time. PPF cannot.
- **Access.** PPF money is locked in: loans are possible from the third year and partial withdrawals from the seventh. A fund can be sold any time, though selling early may cost short-term tax.
- **The PPF rate can change.** The government reviews it every quarter; the comparison holds it at today's rate.
- **The old tax regime.** If you use it, PPF deposits can also cut your tax, which helps PPF further.
- **The limit.** PPF takes at most ₹1.5 lakh a year, so ₹12,500 a month is the most you can put in.

Many people use both: PPF for the part that must be safe, a SIP for the part that can wait out bad years. Try your own amount and rates in the [SIP vs PPF vs RD calculator](/tools/sip-vs-ppf-vs-fd/).
