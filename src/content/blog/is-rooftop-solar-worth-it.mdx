Rooftop solar is an upfront cost that pays you back through smaller electricity bills. Whether it is worth it comes down to three things: what the system costs after any subsidy, how many units it saves you, and what each of those units is worth.

## A made-up example

Take a 3 kW system costing ₹1.8 lakh with no subsidy, on a home that uses 300 units a month at an average of ₹7 a unit. Assume each kW produces 4 units a day and the panels lose 0.5% of their output a year. These are illustrative numbers, not a quote or a forecast.

- The system makes about 3 × 4 × 365 = 4,380 units in the first year, against 3,600 units used.
- So it covers the whole bill: ₹300 × 7 × 12 = ₹25,200 a year.
- ₹1.8 lakh ÷ ₹2,100 a month = **about 86 months, or 7.2 years**, to pay back.
- Over 25 years it saves about ₹6.3 lakh in bills: a net gain of ₹4.5 lakh, a return of about 14% a year.

## Units beyond your own use

In that example the panels make about 22% more than the house uses. Those extra units are only worth something if your electricity company credits them. If it does not, a smaller system could pay back faster. The tool has a field for the credit per exported unit; leave it at zero if you get none.

## Your top slab matters more than your average

Many tariffs bill in slabs, where later blocks of units cost more than earlier ones. Solar cuts the units you buy from the top down, so the first units it saves are your most expensive ones. That is why households with high bills tend to see faster paybacks than the average rate suggests. Once your state's tariff is verified in our data, the tool will bill slab by slab; until then, use your average price per unit from a recent bill.

## What can change the answer

- **The subsidy.** It comes straight off the cost. Check what applies to you before deciding; we have not verified the current amounts yet.
- **Generation.** Shade, roof direction and dust all lower it. Use your installer's estimate for your roof, and be cautious.
- **Tariff rises.** If electricity gets dearer, solar saves more each year. The tool starts at no rise and lets you change it.
- **Inverter replacement.** If you expect to replace the inverter within the period, add the year and the cost your installer quotes.
