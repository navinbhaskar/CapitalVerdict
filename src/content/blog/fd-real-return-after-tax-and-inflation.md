A fixed deposit at 7% feels like a safe 7%. But two things quietly take a share: income tax on the interest every year, and inflation, which shrinks what each rupee buys. What is left is the **real return**, and for many savers it is close to zero or below.

## Step 1: tax on the interest

FD interest is added to your income and taxed every year at your slab rate, plus 4% cess. So a 7% FD grows by 7% × (1 − your tax rate):

- **No tax:** 7%
- **10.4% (the 10% slab with cess):** 6.27%
- **20.8% (the 20% slab with cess):** 5.54%
- **31.2% (the 30% slab with cess):** 4.82%

## Step 2: inflation

If prices rise 6% a year, your money only buys more if it grows faster than 6%. The real return is (1 + return after tax) ÷ 1.06 − 1:

- **No tax:** +0.94% a year
- **10.4%:** +0.26%
- **20.8%:** −0.43%
- **31.2%:** **−1.12%**

At the higher slabs, a 7% FD loses buying power every year, even though its balance keeps growing.

## Over 10 years

₹10 lakh in a 7% FD for 10 years, taxed at 31.2%, grows to about **₹16.01 lakh**. That looks like a good gain. But in today's rupees, at 6% inflation, it is worth about **₹8.94 lakh**: less than you started with. Without tax, it would have grown to ₹19.67 lakh.

## What this means

- **FDs still have a job.** They are safe, easy to cash and right for money you may need soon. Just don't count on them to grow your wealth at the higher slabs.
- **Compare after tax.** PPF at 7.1% is tax-free, so after 6% inflation it stays ahead by about 1.04% a year. Equity funds are taxed only when you sell, and long-term gains above ₹1.25 lakh a year at 12.5%.
- **Inflation is an assumption.** 6% is the middle of the range our tools use; change it if you expect something different.
