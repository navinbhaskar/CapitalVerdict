Plot vs mutual fund: the costs plot buyers forget

A plot's price may double, but stamp duty, holding costs, selling costs and tax take a large share. Compared with an FD and an equity fund, a ₹20 lakh plot needs to grow over 11% a year to keep up.

22 October 2026, 3 min read

In short

  • A ₹20 lakh plot really costs ₹21.6 lakh once stamp duty and fees are paid.
  • Growing 5% a year, it leaves ₹30.08 lakh after 10 years, about 3.37% a year on your money.
  • To match an equity fund at 10% a year, its price would need to grow 11.22% a year.

“Land never loses value” is one of the most repeated pieces of money advice in India. Prices often do rise, but a plot’s headline price rise is not what you keep. Four costs eat into it, and none of them hits an FD or a mutual fund the same way.

The four costs

  1. Stamp duty and registration, paid on day one. On a ₹20 lakh plot at an illustrative 7%, that is ₹1.4 lakh that earns nothing.
  2. Holding costs: fencing, upkeep, property tax. A plot pays you no income while you own it.
  3. Selling costs: brokerage and fees, often a few percent of the sale price.
  4. Capital-gains tax on the gain when you sell, at 12.5% plus cess.

A worked example

A ₹20 lakh plot, with 7% stamp duty and registration and ₹20,000 of other buying costs, so it really costs ₹21.6 lakh. It grows 5% a year, costs ₹5,000 a year to hold and 2% to sell. After 10 years:

  • It sells for ₹32.58 lakh, less ₹65,156 of selling costs, ₹1.34 lakh of tax and ₹50,000 of holding costs. You keep ₹30.08 lakh: about 3.37% a year on the ₹21.6 lakh you put in.

Put the same ₹21.6 lakh elsewhere instead:

  • Fixed deposit at an illustrative 7%, with interest taxed each year at 31.2%: ₹34.57 lakh.
  • Equity mutual fund at an illustrative 10% a year before tax, with long-term capital-gains tax on gains above ₹1.25 lakh when sold: ₹51.71 lakh, about 9.12% a year after tax.

What the plot would need

With these figures, the plot’s price would have to grow 11.22% a year to match the fund. Even at 8% a year, it would leave you ₹39.12 lakh, still well behind. The costs are why: they are paid whether the price rises or not.

None of this means plots are always a bad idea. In a fast-growing area, prices can beat these rates. The point is to compare against the right bar: what your money would leave you elsewhere, after all costs and tax.

What the numbers leave out

  • Selling takes time. A plot can take months to sell; an FD or fund can be cashed in days.
  • Title and boundary risks: disputes and encroachment are real costs for some buyers.
  • Fund returns vary. The 10% here is an illustration, not a promise; an FD’s rate is fixed.
  • Building a home later is a reason to buy a plot that the numbers do not capture.

Stamp duty rates differ by state and change from time to time, so use the rate your registry office or lawyer quotes. Then try your own plot in the plot vs FD vs mutual fund calculator.
